NammaMetro: The Conspiracy Theory

Did BMRCL stage a payments disruption to nudge users toward smart cards? An investigation into Bangalore Metro's payment systems, anchored on a two-day anomaly in January 2025 that the data does not refute.

ByMahesh Shantaram

February 8, 2025

1. One Day on NammaMetro

Let's take a look at the source of data that informs the analysis to follow.

I've been studying ridership trends based on the data that BMRCL puts out daily. While the data tells many stories, it leaves us with some mysteries that only BMRCL can/should solve. I'm putting it out here for public scrutiny.

Take a look at the BMRCL ridership stats page. You get one day's data, (almost) every day, split by payment methods used by passengers entering the system.

A single day's data is of very little use by itself. So I've been collecting this over time and have made it publichttps://github.com/thecont1/namma-metro-ridership-tracker/tree/main/scrolly-article/. It's open data for one and all.

The dataset at a glance

Let's take a subset of the dataset ranging from November 16, 2024 to April 14, 2025 for the purpose of our discussion. Each cell below is a day in this editorial window . Rows are days of the week — Monday at the top to Sunday at the bottom. Darker purple = more riders; crossed cells = days when BMRCL didn't publish.

120 days, laid out as a Monday-to-Sunday calendar.
Colour = total daily ridership. Intensity = percentile. Crossed cells = missing data.

If it looks like a mouth with a few broken teeth, it's because every now and then, NammaMetro fails to report ridership numbers. This usually happens when the individual tasked with manually updating the data page goes on leave.

What sort of data imputation makes sense here? Since the dataset is large enough, it makes sense to fill unreported dates with an average of similar days, e.g. If a weekday Tuesday was missing, we would take the average of the last 4-6 available weekday Tuesdays.

A single day by how we pay

Here's a treemap of 7 payment modes sized by share of the day's total. Smart Cards (the closed-loop channel) still account for the majority of rides but the convenience of QR is getting more and more popular. NCMC usage hardly registers but with a push from the top – the prime minister was in the city recently to launch user — this is bound to increase.

2. “The Metro is Getting Crowded!”

Ridership is setting new growth records, and that is a good thing. But office-goers in the daily crush hour are asking, “How many is way too many?”

In October 2023, NammaMetro's single-day ridership hit a record of 700,000 passengers.https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/bengaluru/with-entire-purple-line-operational-metro-ridership-crosses-7-lakh-mark/articleshow/104384854.cms On December 10, 2024, that number crossed 900,000 for the first time. It took just a little over a year to add 200,000 riders/day to the system.

The 10 busiest and 10 least busy days in the editorial window, ranked together.

The busiest days are regular workdays – Manic Mondays, Terrible Tuesdays, Wasted Wednesdays. Sundays, holidays, and major festivals (like Sankranti on Jan 14 ) are when people take a break—and Metro gets a break.

3. One Week on NammaMetro

From late October to early February, the metro carried about 8 lakh riders a day. The rhythm was that of a city that works hard and plays hard.

Ridership has a strong weekly cycle. Weekdays look like weekdays; weekends look like weekends. The interesting story is which channels dominate on which days.

From Monday to Thursday, Smart Card and NCMC usage — typically used by regular metro riders commuting to work — averages ~450,000 and ~12,000 respectively. Tokens and QR payments, typically used by non-regular metro riders, reach peak usage on Sundays. Weekend and holiday demand is driven by casual users — road users who switch to public transport on the weekends.

4. Three Traffic Bands, Two Kinds of Patrons, One Metro

Ridership starts to dip on Fridays (perhaps due to liberal work-from-office policies in the IT industry or commuters preferring to use their personal vehicles.) For another segment of workers, Saturdays are as much a working day as the rest of the week.

On Sundays, Smart Cards+NCMC usage sees a ~60% drop from average Weekday levels. Tokens+QR increases by ~15%. This confirms the presence of two distinct types of users: Commuters and Casual Users.

💡 Three Traffic Bands — Weekday (Mon–Thu), Weekend Lite (Fri–Sat), Weekend (Sun). The visualisation validates the hypothesis about Bangalore's distinct traffic bands and rider categories.

👆🏼 Surprising find: Smart Card and NCMC usage peaks during the week; Tokens and QR peaks on weekends.

💡 Two Kinds of Patrons — NammaMetro thus exhibits a dual role: a commuter necessity on weekdays, and a weekend leisure service.

5. One Month on NammaMetro

Remember that NammaMetro fails to report ridership numbers on some days, so the monthly ridership totals calculated from the data are only approximate. However, we can still identify trends and patterns by analysing data movement on a granular level.

The plot below reveals strong weekly cycles — each weekend dip is followed by a sharp recovery, demonstrating the metro's vital role in weekday commuting. The high variability in aggregate data suggests we should segment our analysis to reveal patterns currently masked by day-to-day fluctuations.

6. The Long Weekend and Other Phenomena

Holidays and cultural events have dramatically different impacts on different user segments. The separate but complementary views of Commute and Casual ridership expose patterns that would be completely hidden in the aggregate data.

Sankranti and Something Else

Inverse Relationship: The separate ridership trends for Casual and Commute users confirm a pattern we've observed before — when Commuters drop, Casual riders rise. However, there are moments — like Sankranti — when this balance breaks down.

📘 Notes — The Sankranti week (Jan 14–19, 2025) shows a striking anomaly. On Wednesday Jan 15, the Token system appears to have experienced a sudden drop; on Thursday Jan 16, the QR (NammaMetro) channel collapses while Smart Card surges correspondingly. We'll return to this in Chapter 9.

7. Metro Enables the Visitor Economy

Bangalore is a rising destination for major sporting and entertainment events, and the city's public and private infrastructure is constantly being upgraded to support these industries.

On Jan 25, Karnataka played (and won!) against Punjab in the Ranji Trophy at Bangalore's (metro-accessible) Chinnaswamy Stadium. A couple of weeks later, Ed Sheeran enthralled audiences at the (metro-accessible) NICE Grounds. The spike in 1/3/5 day passes suggests that the city welcomed a wave of visitors who relied on the metro to get around.

8. Fare Hike of February 2025 — Impact Analysis

Following recommendations of a fare fixation committee, BMRCL revised its fares for the first time since 2017. The new fares took effect on February 9, 2025.https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/bangalore/after-bus-fare-hike-bengalureans-will-now-have-to-shell-out-more-for-namma-metro-rides-from-feb-9/article69196596.ece

The decision proved to be hugely unpopular with regular metro users, rudely surprising many commuters at the turnstiles on Monday morning, as well as public transportation activists.https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/bangalore/metro-ridership-drop-bmrcl-plug-fare-hike-public-backlash-9830375/

Did it hurt?

To assess the immediate impact of the fare hike, we analyzed ridership trends over a six-week window — three weeks before and after the fare revision on February 9, 2025.

✅ Ridership decline is evident. The trendline suggests a sustained drop rather than a random fluctuation. 📉 Sharp immediate fall. In the week following the fare hike, ridership plunged significantly, suggesting a strong commuter response to the price change.

Who did it hurt?

The drop in weekday ridership was primarily driven by daily commuters who reduced their Metro usage more significantly than casual riders. Casual ridership showed some resilience, likely due to weekend trips and one-off events. Smart Card users remain the majority, but the decline in token-based payments suggests infrequent riders reduced trips.

A drop in QR and NCMC-based transactions indicates a potential pullback from occasional users. (The NCMC disruption in March 2025 is a separate incident, documented in news reports.https://www.timesnownews.com/bengaluru/why-has-bengalurus-namma-metro-halted-ncmc-cards-services-article-151381010)

How badly did it hurt?

To quantify the impact, we conducted statistical hypothesis testing at a 99% confidence level for weekdays and a 95% confidence level for weekends.

🛜 NCMC Growth Amid Decline — NCMC usage surged +39.4% (CI: +33.0% to +45.7%) across both Weekday and Weekend Lite bands while all other payment methods declined. 🚇 Smart Card Decline Severe — Despite the 5-10% discount benefit, Smart Card use dropped by 9.4% (CI: -12.2% to -6.6%).https://bangaloremirror.indiatimes.com/bangalore/others/glitch-in-the-metro-matrix/articleshow/124312799.cms

9. The Conspiracy Theory 😈

So far we've established the system, the patterns, the post-fare-hike impact. Now we ask the harder question: was the shift toward Smart Cards the inevitable result of the hike, or was there a quieter nudge already underway?

The Curious Case of Jan 15–16

Let's start with what is plainly visible in the data.

  1. In any normal week, Smart Cards, QR, and Token payments average 450K, 190K, and 200K respectively.
  2. On Wednesday, January 15, when people were getting back to work after an extended Sankranti holiday, something disrupted the Token system, causing a sharp drop to just 84K transactions — less than half the usual.
  3. The very next day, Thursday January 16, the QR payment system collapsed to 12K — accompanied by an astronomical surge in Smart Cards to 631K.

👆🏼 Jan 15: Notice the sudden drop in Tokens and sharp rise in Smart Cards.
👇🏼 Jan 16: A collapse in QR payments is accompanied by an astronomical surge in Smart Cards.

Payment Disruptions: Examining the Patterns

Before drawing conclusions, let's lay out the key observations and assumptions.

  1. Highly Unusual Sequence of Events — A breakdown in the Token system on January 15, immediately followed by a QR payment failure on January 16, is an unusual sequence. Both disruptions resolved fully and immediately after just one day, which is not typical of independent technical failures, where we'd expect staggered recovery times. The likelihood of two unrelated failures occurring on consecutive days is low.
  2. Migration, not Abandonment — Smart Card usage didn't drop during this period; it surged. This suggests that riders migrated from Token and QR to Smart Card, rather than abandoning the metro entirely.

Putting the Hypothesis to the Test

To confirm whether these changes were natural variations or an engineered shift, we put our hypothesis to a statistically rigorous test.

Key Findings

  • Token usage dropped sharply (-10.7%, CI: -13.2% to -8.2%) exactly when Smart Cards surged (+9.7%, CI: +5.3% to +14.1%).
  • This isn't just a random fluctuation — it indicates a systematic shift in commuter behavior.
  • Interestingly, QR payments show a significant uptick (+15.1%) only during Weekend Lite periods, while NCMC transactions saw strong growth across the board.

🤔 The Unanswered Questions

  • Was this a deliberate nudge to increase Smart Card adoption in the lead-up to a fare hike?
  • Or was it a series of unrelated technical failures that happened to align in a way that benefited Smart Cards?
  • The data shows the what. It does not, on its own, show the why.
