1. One Day on NammaMetro

Let's take a look at the source of data that informs the analysis to follow.

I've been studying ridership trends based on the data that BMRCL puts out daily. While the data tells many stories, it leaves us with some mysteries that only BMRCL can/should solve. I'm putting it out here for public scrutiny.

Take a look at the BMRCL ridership stats page. You get one day's data, (almost) every day, split by payment methods used by passengers entering the system.

A single day's data is of very little use by itself. So I've been collecting this over time and have made it publichttps://github.com/thecont1/namma-metro-ridership-tracker/tree/main/scrolly-article/. It's open data for one and all.

The dataset at a glance

Let's take a subset of the dataset ranging from November 16, 2024 to April 14, 2025 for the purpose of our discussion. Each cell below is a day in this editorial window . Rows are days of the week — Monday at the top to Sunday at the bottom. Darker purple = more riders; crossed cells = days when BMRCL didn't publish.

120 days, laid out as a Monday-to-Sunday calendar.
Colour = total daily ridership. Intensity = percentile. Crossed cells = missing data.

If it looks like a mouth with a few broken teeth, it's because every now and then, NammaMetro fails to report ridership numbers. This usually happens when the individual tasked with manually updating the data page goes on leave.

What sort of data imputation makes sense here? Since the dataset is large enough, it makes sense to fill unreported dates with an average of similar days, e.g. If a weekday Tuesday was missing, we would take the average of the last 4-6 available weekday Tuesdays.
