9. The Conspiracy Theory 😈

So far we've established the system, the patterns, the post-fare-hike impact. Now we ask the harder question: was the shift toward Smart Cards the inevitable result of the hike, or was there a quieter nudge already underway?

The Curious Case of Jan 15–16

Let's start with what is plainly visible in the data.

  1. In any normal week, Smart Cards, QR, and Token payments average 450K, 190K, and 200K respectively.
  2. On Wednesday, January 15, when people were getting back to work after an extended Sankranti holiday, something disrupted the Token system, causing a sharp drop to just 84K transactions — less than half the usual.
  3. The very next day, Thursday January 16, the QR payment system collapsed to 12K — accompanied by an astronomical surge in Smart Cards to 631K.

👆🏼 Jan 15: Notice the sudden drop in Tokens and sharp rise in Smart Cards.
👇🏼 Jan 16: A collapse in QR payments is accompanied by an astronomical surge in Smart Cards.

Payment Disruptions: Examining the Patterns

Before drawing conclusions, let's lay out the key observations and assumptions.

  1. Highly Unusual Sequence of Events — A breakdown in the Token system on January 15, immediately followed by a QR payment failure on January 16, is an unusual sequence. Both disruptions resolved fully and immediately after just one day, which is not typical of independent technical failures, where we'd expect staggered recovery times. The likelihood of two unrelated failures occurring on consecutive days is low.
  2. Migration, not Abandonment — Smart Card usage didn't drop during this period; it surged. This suggests that riders migrated from Token and QR to Smart Card, rather than abandoning the metro entirely.

Putting the Hypothesis to the Test

To confirm whether these changes were natural variations or an engineered shift, we put our hypothesis to a statistically rigorous test.

Key Findings

  • Token usage dropped sharply (-10.7%, CI: -13.2% to -8.2%) exactly when Smart Cards surged (+9.7%, CI: +5.3% to +14.1%).
  • This isn't just a random fluctuation — it indicates a systematic shift in commuter behavior.
  • Interestingly, QR payments show a significant uptick (+15.1%) only during Weekend Lite periods, while NCMC transactions saw strong growth across the board.

🤔 The Unanswered Questions

  • Was this a deliberate nudge to increase Smart Card adoption in the lead-up to a fare hike?
  • Or was it a series of unrelated technical failures that happened to align in a way that benefited Smart Cards?
  • The data shows the what. It does not, on its own, show the why.
